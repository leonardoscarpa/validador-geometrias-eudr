Same land, different answers.

Compliance screening for Regulation (EU) 2023/1115 leans on public satellite datasets. I ran four of them over the same six areas of about 48,000 hectares. Most of the area flagged after the 31 December 2020 cutoff is flagged by only one dataset. This page shows where they disagree, why, and what a compliance team can do about it.

Results from one run on six synthetic test boxes (22 October 2026). The boxes are not real suppliers and what is grown inside each one is not verified. Nothing here is a compliance verdict, and disagreement does not show which dataset is right.

Why they disagree

Each dataset answers a different question, and none of them answers the legal one directly: was this forest, as defined in Article 2, converted to agricultural use after 31 December 2020?

Definitions

Article 2 defines forest as land of more than 0.5 ha with trees over 5 m and canopy cover over 10%, excluding land predominantly under agricultural use. Agricultural plantations, including oil palm and agroforestry with crops under tree cover, are excluded from forest. Hansen measures tree cover loss, so it also fires on harvest and renewal of plantations. In the cocoa box only 3% of Hansen loss falls on land the JRC map calls forest at 2020, against 96% in Misiones.

Wording read from a secondary copy of the consolidated text. Check against EUR-Lex before citing.

Resolution and dates

Datasets run at 10 m or 30 m, so small clearings can be seen by one and missed by another. Hansen and TMF report a year, not a day, so a 2020 flag cannot say whether the clearing came before or after 31 December. This run treats 2020 as before the cutoff.

Coverage

RADD only monitors primary humid tropical forest: 0 ha in Misiones and Côte d'Ivoire, 12 to 19% of the other boxes. The commodity probability models only apply between 24 degrees north and south, so not in Misiones. A dataset that does not cover the plot is silent, not clean.

Thresholds

Lowering Hansen's tree cover threshold from 30% to 10% adds 1 to 6% more flagged area in most boxes, but 49% more in the cocoa box, where tree cover is sparse and mixed with crops.

What to do when they disagree

A proposed decision protocol. Pick a situation.

This is a methodological proposal, not legal advice and not an official procedure. For every plot, record the datasets and versions, the run date, the thresholds and which datasets cover the plot. The stakes are asymmetric: a false positive can cost a commercial relationship, a false negative can cost fines of at least 4% of EU turnover (Art. 25), and under Art. 9 any deforestation or degradation on a plot disqualifies its products.

Limits, stated up front

  • Six synthetic boxes and one run. This is not a statistical study of dataset accuracy.
  • The commodity in each box is a regional label. It was not verified.
  • There is no ground truth here, so disagreement does not say which dataset is right.
  • Misiones: Hansen may be recording harvest of planted pine, which Article 2 does not treat as deforestation if the land stays forest. That is a hypothesis. These datasets cannot tell harvest from conversion.
  • RADD dates are assumed to be year and day of year. The raw ranges in the output fit that, but it was checked by plausibility, not documentation.
  • The commodity models give probabilities, not observations, and there is no soy or wood model.
  • JRC also publishes a forest subtype layer (primary, naturally regenerating, planted). It was not used and would be the next check for Misiones.
