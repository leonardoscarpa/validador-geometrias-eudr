What this demonstrates Automated screening for EUDR Article 9/10 supplier due diligence. Each plot is checked against satellite change-detection data for the 31 Dec 2020 cutoff (not a single snapshot), with no tolerance threshold, and degradation counted alongside deforestation for wood (Article 2).

The plot near Iguazú failed the EUDR cutoff check, while the province with the worst deforestation record passed.

Three test plots, checked pixel-by-pixel against the EUDR's 31 December 2020 cutoff date, using live satellite data processed in Google Earth Engine. A 24,800-hectare box near Iguazú, in a province known for its forest cover, came back flagged. A 100,000-hectare box in Santiago del Estero, Argentina's most-deforested province in every annual report from 2021 to 2025, came back clean.

This doesn't prove Santiago del Estero is deforestation-free: it proves regional reputation alone isn't enough for an Article 10 risk assessment. Full methodology below.

The three test plots

Each one evaluated against two independent JRC Tropical Moist Forest layers: deforestation year and degradation year. A single post-2020 pixel in either layer disqualifies the whole plot, with no tolerance threshold.

How this was calculated

Why the 2020 snapshot isn't enough

JRC/GFC2020/V3 is a single fixed snapshot at 31/12/2020: it confirms forest was there that day, but can't say whether it was cleared afterward. That requires a change layer: JRC TMF v1_2025, which records the exact year of deforestation and degradation, pixel by pixel, since 1990.

Why degradation is checked, not just deforestation

Article 2 requires that wood specifically be "harvested without inducing forest degradation" after the cutoff. Standing forest isn't enough on its own. Most demos out there only look at deforestation. This one checks both layers.

No tolerance threshold

Article 9 disqualifies automatically at any trace of deforestation or degradation: there's no tolerated percentage of affected area. The script mirrors that rule exactly: a single pixel is enough.

What "no coverage" means

TMF covers the tropical moist forest domain. Where it has no classification at all, the script doesn't silently default to "compliant": it returns an explicit third state flagging the need for another data source. None of the three test plots hit that case.

Methodological honesty note: "Compliant" here means "no change detected within this specific box," not a certification that the whole region is deforestation-free. A test box, however large, may not land on the exact site of an actual clearing event. This screening reduces risk, it doesn't eliminate it, which is exactly why Products 1 and 3 of this portfolio exist, to document the rest of the due diligence process.
